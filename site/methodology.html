Methodology

Every figure in Tend is an estimate. AI providers don't publish enough infrastructure data to calculate the energy, carbon, or water of a single request exactly. Tend uses published research and provider disclosures to calculate a low, middle, and high estimate, and shows the range.

How the estimate is calculated

Tend counts four kinds of tokens from Claude Code's local usage logs: generated output, fresh input, cache writes, and cached input. Each kind gets an energy factor, then:

energy  = Σ (tokens × Wh per token) × data-center overhead (PUE)
carbon  = energy × grid carbon intensity
water   = energy × water per kWh
range   = middle estimate × 0.4 (low) … × 2.5 (high)

Cached input makes up most of a coding agent's tokens, so the cached-input factor matters most and is the least certain. These factors will change as better measurements become public.

What we don't know

For any single request, we usually don't know:

  • which hardware handled it
  • the exact energy it consumed
  • which data center ran it
  • that facility's cooling system
  • the electricity mix at that moment
  • the facility's water consumption

Tend shows that uncertainty openly, as a range around each number.

What funding means

Paying for carbon removal or water restoration doesn't make the original footprint disappear. Tend records what you estimated, what you funded, and whatever evidence the provider gives you.

Water is local. Restoration in one watershed supports an equivalent amount; it doesn't replace water used somewhere else.

What Tend reads

Tend runs on your computer. It reads token counts, model names, timestamps, and the projects you choose to track from Claude Code's local logs. It doesn't read your prompts, code, files, or project contents. It sends nothing anywhere, unless you choose to add a purchase to Tend's shared total, which sends only that purchase's amounts and proof link.

Why this matters

AI should run without burning fossil fuels or using up fresh water, and every Tend estimate measures the distance from that standard. Most of AI's water is used at power plants: US data centers consumed about 17 billion gallons directly in 2023, and their electricity used roughly 211 billion more (Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory). Hourly carbon-free power on the local grid and sealed cooling that evaporates nothing would solve both. Until providers publish energy, carbon, and water for every request, Tend shows a range, and this method changes when better data arrives. Ask providers to publish it →

Changelog

Tend reviews these factors every quarter and updates them when a factor changes or a major new source is published. Each footprint file and each recorded purchase stores the method version used. After an update, a funded badge keeps its check for 30 days so you can fund any difference in the new estimate.
